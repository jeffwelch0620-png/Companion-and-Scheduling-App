# Bert’s FOH Close — Current Operating Map and Approved J Max Decisions

**Source:** Direct working-session input from Jason Albert, Rudd Maxwell, and Bert’s FOH leadership, August 28, 2026.  
**Status:** Current operating truth and approved product-direction decisions. This document does **not** authorize implementation, policy publication, or personnel action.

## Blunt Operating Finding

The problem is not that Bert’s lacks side work. The problem is that **a manager becomes the verification bottleneck** during banking and books, while employees can leave after work that may not be truly ready for the next shift. J Max must make ownership, physical PASS/FIX, and handoff visible—without replacing service with phone work or turning close into a pile of meaningless clicks.

> **Controlling close rule:** An assigned employee does not self-release based on a completion click. A PASS means an authorized person physically looked.

## Normal FOH Close Sequence

Cleans most tea urns, hot-fudge counter, salad-bar cooler, and salad-bar prep tables. | The work must remain service-safe and normally take about 10–15 minutes, not more than roughly 10% of the shift. Closing manager physically PASSes it before release. Hot fudge remains out and is handed off to a later closer. |
| **Second-out server** | Closes the Back Room and completes any remaining Server Station task before leaving. | Back Room follows the standard section close and senior/lead first verification when available, then final manager walk. Completing a late station task is not self-approval of Server Station. |
| **Named Server Station closer** | Owns the complete Server Station close: beverage equipment, opening stock, counters/floors/trash, and no personal items left. | A designated senior/shift lead physically PASSes or FIXes first; closing manager final-confirms. When staffing changes, a replacement inherits the close. If the team stays short, the manager redistributes labor but names one accountable closer. |
| **Final Salad Bar closer** | Finishes all food protection, labeling/dating, fill, sanitation, covers, crackers, and leak-condition work. | Closing-manager-only thorough checklist and PASS/FIX. In-shift hosts/salad-bar staff maintain general cleanliness but do not provide final approval. |
| **Final Bathroom closer** | Brings bathrooms to the specific cleanliness and stocking standard. | Designated senior/lead physical PASS/FIX first; closing manager final-confirms. |
| **First available of final two closers** | Mops the front-door area. | The task is unassigned until one of the two remaining closers becomes available and claims it. Closing manager physically PASSes it before that server is released. |

## Area Standards Already Defined

| Area | Definition of done |
|---|---|
| **Bathrooms** | Toilets clean; counters and mirrors clean; soap at least half full; two toilet-paper rolls per stall; active paper-towel roll replaced at about one-third; floors swept/mopped and free of dirt; faucets fully off with no drip. |
| **Server Station** | Tea urns cleaned inside/out and drying; soda and tea nozzles soaking; coffee pot cleaned; filters washed/returned; ice bins filled; cups, soufflé cups, lids, sanitation buckets, and bagged silverware ready; trash removed; counters/floors clean; cups put away; no personal items left. |
| **Salad Bar** | Product labeled/dated, filled, protected, and covered; crocks, top bar, glass, sneeze guard, storage/under-crock areas, crackers, and table clean; crumbs removed; condensation/leaks checked. |
| **Back Window** | Employee restroom cleaned; pizza boxes folded; backup dressings/condiments ready; window locked; table reset; area clean and swept/mopped. Cash drawer is separately manager-counted. |
| **Server section / Back Room** | Tables wiped/reset, floors swept/mopped, and area generally clean. Back Room is simply a separate party/overflow room, not a special checklist. |
| **Host / entry area** | Booster seats and high chairs clean; menus wiped; glass/front doors cleaned and wiped; weekend Back Room support complete when assigned. |

## Verification and Correction Model

| Stage | Required behavior |
|---|---|
| **Assignment** | Close area is assigned during schedule creation based on section, start time, and close role. Day-of information reinforces the assignment. |
| **Ready signal** | Owner signals ready only after actual work is complete. |
| **Evidence** | J Max requests a broad reset view plus rotating, unpredictable targeted photo proof where photos are used. The closer uploads as many current photos as necessary to show the requested condition; no fixed photo quota. Photos support management review and never replace a physical check. |
| **First PASS/FIX** | Senior/lead approval is permitted only where explicitly granted for the shift and zone. Salad Bar and Back Window remain manager-PASS areas. |
| **Correction** | A FIX returns first to the original assigned closer where practical. A qualified person who actually helped may be assigned when needed; J Max records both the original owner and correction completer. |
| **Manager signal** | Every Server Station FIX is visible to the closing manager. Manager acknowledgment is required only for repeated, serious, or unresolved misses. |
| **Late miss** | If final walk reveals a miss after the original employee is released, the manager owns a late exception. The remaining team corrects it that night; the original missed standard remains visible for transparent coaching, never hidden scoring or automatic discipline. |
| **Photo retention** | Normal passing close photos are visible only to the closer, verifier, and authorized management. They disappear after final confirmation or at 48 hours maximum. Exception evidence exists only while follow-up requires. |

## Closing Manager Final Walk and Security Handoff

Once books are complete and funds are secured for safe/deposit, the manager validates sections and side work, locked entrances/windows/Back Window/back door, HVAC settings, office/kitchen/front-light status, front exit doors, and parking-lot debris/trash. The kitchen manager hands off the shed as ready; the closing manager confirms the shed door is locked, interior lights are off, and exterior spotlights are on. If the kitchen manager is unavailable, the closing manager may complete the task as a recorded exception.

A safely unresolved maintenance, security, or facility issue becomes a severity-based exception. The closing manager decides whether a professional is needed that night or the matter can be prioritized first thing the next morning. J Max may organize the evidence, contacts, and follow-up, but cannot authorize spend or dispatch a vendor. It automatically alerts an owner that night for safety/security concerns or any issue requiring an after-hours professional call.

For non-emergency issues deferred to morning, the closing manager retains responsibility until the issue is explicitly handed off to the opening manager. The receiving manager must acknowledge the handoff before or at the start of their shift.

## Design Guardrails

J Max should reduce verification friction, not create a digital version of a 40-item clipboard. Managers need clear exceptions and timely handoffs, not nuisance prompts. Servers need an explicit definition of done and a fair chance to correct work while still present. The result to measure later is fewer preventable next-shift and guest-facing surprises—not higher completion-click volume.
