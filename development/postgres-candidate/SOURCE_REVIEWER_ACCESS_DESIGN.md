## Purpose and prerequisites

Replace supplied offline target/access claims with a verified, scoped server read when the shared identity foundation is ready. First define who can review which source families, how owners administer those designations, and how a read detects current revocation. The existing V3 CLI remains an offline comparison accepting supplied evidence; its accessFence and reviewer labels are never authentication.

The [shared identity design](SHARED_EMPLOYEE_IDENTITY_ACCESS_DESIGN.md), [ingestion policy](SOURCE_INGESTION_DESIGN.md) and [target-read contract](SOURCE_NORMALIZATION_TARGET_READ_DESIGN.md) govern this package. The shared schema/migration owner, exact tested Inventory baseline/catalog (I06), verified personal account links/session policy and applicable I07–I09 decisions remain dependencies. This design can be reviewed now; it does not authorize their implementation or invent a replacement shared authz service in candidate_identity.

## Logical designation and authority model

These are proposed logical records, not approved DDL/table names:

- Identify a designation by organization, canonical store, person and source family (roster or timeOff), with a stable UUID, revision, capability level (review or review_and_certify), captured eligibilityRevision, effective state and person-level author/revoker attribution. The review level permits source.review only; review_and_certify additionally permits source.certify. Validate canonical store ownership and supported family; no wildcard organization/all-families designation for ordinary reviewers.
- Keep review designation separate from employment, provider aliases, operational grants, owner seats and the administrator switch. An organization-wide owner/admin reads through current organization authority; enabling/disabling that status never rewrites reviewer history or creates employment.
- A grant, level-change, restore or revoke-other command must derive the actor from verified server identity, require current owner/enabled-admin authority, compare actor/target person IDs, require a reason and expected designation revision, and retain before/after state. Changing review to review_and_certify requires an explicit owner/admin decision and a new audited designation revision; a generic designation never implies certification. A repeated identical receipt returns one result/audit event; changed payload under the same ID conflicts. Semantic no-ops do not advance access revisions or duplicate events.
- Re-granting a revoked designation is an explicit new audited transition with a new revision; account/person reactivation must not silently restore a designation invalidated by loss of eligibility. Ordinary logout/session expiry only ends that session, not the durable designation; a new verified eligible session may use an otherwise valid designation. Ending or reactivating employment alone neither revokes nor creates this independently designated reviewer authority under selected R04. Explicit revocation ends the designation; a person/account/organization/store eligibility lapse instead invalidates its captured eligibility revision and blocks access without automatically setting the designation to revoked. Preserve that lapse evidence and require a fresh owner/admin designation review before restoring access.
- Designation changes can never edit owners, turn the administrator switch on, bind a login or delegate designation management. Do not derive identity-administration rights from certification eligibility. General delegation/bootstrap/recovery under I07–I09 remain separate.
- A schedule-only membership or roster record alone cannot authenticate or establish source-review authority. An independently verified active person may receive the explicit designation without employment under R04; never convert a roster record into an account or treat schedule-only membership as that designation. A disabled/suspended person, invalid/unlinked session, inactive store or organization mismatch denies access regardless of retained historical designation.
- A timeOff-only designation cannot read a roster directory. If resolving that family needs unavailable person/member information, return an unavailable/unresolved affected review rather than broadening authority. Historical time-off departments remain unchanged.
- Source/target alias review can inspect approved links within scope. An authorized owner/admin initiates person-candidate proposals through the separate identity workflow; that workflow supplies only approved, minimal scoped candidate references for this review context. A source reviewer may confirm one of those candidates but cannot propose arbitrary person UUIDs, enumerate a directory, create/rebind an identity alias or approve membership through source.review/source.certify. If no approved candidate is supplied, linking remains unresolved. Selecting a matching name never creates an alias.

The proposed implementation capability names are source.review for bounded target/mapping review and source.certify for future completeness certification. The selected distinction is explicit in the designation level: review by default, review_and_certify only when explicitly approved. Both use the same shared policy and organization/store/family scope, with no shortcut around R01–R08. Owners/currently enabled admin retain their selected organization-policy eligibility without a dummy designation. R07 permits author self-certification for any otherwise authorized certifier; record the supplement/mapping authors, certifying person, exact context digest and decision in the future audit. Authorship alone grants no authority and overrides no validation. Designation management is the protected owner/enabled-admin predicate, except audited R08 self-revocation; no ordinary grantable source.reviewer.manage shortcut. No capability or grant is implemented here. Application requires its own separately designed permission and command, even for owners.

## Owner oversight and self-revocation

The future owner/enabled-admin read model must list every current designation in the organization, including non-employed reviewers, review-only and certification levels, and records currently ineligible because their captured revision is stale. Return store, family, person UUID/current permitted name, capability level, granted/restored by, grant time, designation revision, captured/current eligibility revisions and current eligibility status. Separate authorized history can show revoked records. Bound/paginate consistently; never silently omit designations from the owner's list or expose it through ordinary source.review. No employee directory, contact/payroll fields or login secrets are included.

Create an advisory item for current organization owners when a designee's last active approved membership ends or their person becomes inactive. A membership ending is advisory only: it does not change the designation, capability level or eligibilityRevision under R04/R05. Person inactivity also creates an advisory, while its eligibility lapse immediately blocks access through the counter rule below. Deduplicate advisory items by designation plus lifecycle event/revision so replay does not create duplicates. An advisory is an owner review obligation, not an automatic revoke, expiry, downgrade or claim of notification delivery; actual delivery remains separate work.

For R08, a currently verified eligible person may revoke only an explicit designation whose person UUID matches their own, with reason, expected revision, receipt/replay checks and an audit event. This narrow relinquishment path requires no certification or designation-administration capability and may be used even when that designation's captured eligibility is stale, provided the actor's current personal identity/session is valid. An optional minimal own-designation read supports relinquishment without disclosing source rows. It cannot grant, restore, change levels, affect another person or relinquish organization-wide owner/admin authority. Replayed self-revocation creates no second event; restore requires a different authorized owner/admin and cannot be self-approved.

## Durable eligibility revisions

At grant or explicit restore, capture eligibilityRevision as a vector of application-owned monotonic person, account-link, organization and store eligibility counters, after validating their current eligible state. Every person disable/suspension, account-link disable/unlink/rebind, or organization/store eligibility lapse advances the relevant counter transactionally through its shared lifecycle write function. Re-enable never decrements a counter or refreshes existing designations. Grant/restore explicitly records the current vector, reviewer, reason and new designation revision. No employment-membership counter participates under R04. Owners/enabled admin who act through organization policy use current organization/session eligibility and access fences, without a fabricated designation or captured designation vector.

For designation-based authority, both the initial authorization and separate final current-state check require active designation/current eligibility and stored eligibilityRevision == current eligibilityRevision, in addition to the before/after access fence. A lapse followed by re-enable remains refused until a fresh authorized designation review captures the new vector; matching before/after active booleans or starting a new read after re-enable cannot bypass it. Ordinary logout/session expiry denies that session but does not advance the durable eligibility counter; a new verified session may use an otherwise valid designation.

For provider-side account/link eligibility lapses, the trusted server identity-lifecycle adapter owns advancing the application counter through coordinated, idempotent shared lifecycle functions. Authenticated provider lifecycle events or authoritative reconciliation feed that adapter; no custom trigger is attached to auth.sessions. Before allowing access after provider reactivation/relinking, the adapter must have established lifecycle continuity and persisted any intervening lapse. Stale, missing or unverifiable provider lifecycle evidence fails closed with no target data until reconciled and, if continuity cannot be proved, a new invalidation revision plus fresh designation review. Never claim that checking a currently valid JWT alone detects every earlier provider lapse. Provider-side disable/re-enable and an event/reconciliation gap are required integration tests before exposure.

## Minimal server contract and projection

The future trusted backend accepts only requested organization/store, reviewed provider namespace/families, supported leave dates and explicit alias references. It derives person/session/authority and authoritative revisions on the server. Refuse the whole requested unauthorized scope without partial rows or store-existence/count leakage. Limit scopes before reading; do not accept caller-supplied target people, owner flags or accessFence as proof.

| Projection | Read rule |
| --- | --- |
| Store | Exact configured timezone, department/job keys and relevant configuration revision; no financial settings or provider secrets. |
| Roster | Complete authorized store membership scope needed for reconciliation, including relevant inactive/schedule-only targets, person/member UUIDs, current names, revisions, department/position and scheduling jobs. A filtered active-only provider source does not hide retained inactive target members. |
| People | Only explicitly mapped and authorized identities needed by that scope. A missing directory permission leaves linking unresolved; it does not enumerate organization employees or guess a person. |
| Aliases | Only reviewed organization/system/tenant/external-store/kind/source-ID links and revisions required by this read. Preserve exact source spelling; duplicate identity comparisons retain the accepted case-insensitive UUID rules. No login/contact/recovery data. |
| Leave | Only authorized overlapping intervals in the requested range, IDs/revisions, owner membership, original department and supported status. No medical detail, reason or attachments. Unsupported target cancellation states make the family unavailable pending its reviewed port; never drop them and imply completeness. |
| Context | Exact relevant source-input/configuration/alias/identity revisions and an opaque snapshot/context digest. Workspace revision may be metadata, never the source-read fence. The actor's verified access/session fence is separate. |

A store reviewer does not receive another store's directory or leave records because one person works in both stores. Future application uses a separately authorized minimal person-wide conflict projection, not another store's roster under the source-review grant. Unavailable evidence prevents later application without expanding disclosure.

Source input remains subject to the V3 recomputation, provider-only sourceVersion, population-bound supplement and full-store selection review. Supplement/reviewer changes stale the review context without rewriting provider fingerprints. A provider name/job change changes sourceVersion and requires alias re-review. Never overwrite a target on the basis of a content hash or manufacture current target revisions.

## Read protocol and access fences

1. Verify the personal provider session through the shared server boundary, resolve a current person and organization, validate every requested store/family, and capture the relevant current access fence before private reads.
2. Assemble all target rows, aliases, relevant context and access evidence in one bounded REPEATABLE READ, READ ONLY transaction. Use immutable-ID ordering. Reads need no store coordination or FOR UPDATE locks; privileged fixture clients are not an authorization layer.
3. Commit that transaction. In a separate short READ COMMITTED transaction, re-resolve current session/person/owner-admin/designation eligibility and compare its fence with the initial one. A fence includes changes to session/link/person state, owner/admin policy, relevant designation, store state and any explicit eligibility dependency. Store employment is not such a dependency under selected R04. When authority comes from a designation, require its captured eligibilityRevision to equal current counters as well as its active state; compare the separate before/after access fence. Monotonic lapse counters prevent revoke/re-enable or disable/re-enable from producing an ABA match, even on a later fresh request.
4. If authorization is invalid or that access fence moved, discard the complete assembled result; return a fixed refusal/refresh outcome without target fragments. Never check current access through the old repeatable-read snapshot. A revocation committed before this final access check must be detected. This cannot retract already delivered data or guarantee detection of a commit after the final check; every subsequent read/command rechecks current authority.
5. Relevant source-input, target/person/alias/configuration changes require a coherent refresh rather than combining snapshots. A proposed technical safeguard permits at most three total whole-read attempts (initial plus two retries) for relevant data drift; this is not an owner-selected production bound. Recheck access every attempt and stop immediately on a true denial. Exhaustion returns HTTP 503 with fixed code source_review_refresh_required and retryable:true, with no target/alias rows or partial report; the operator must refresh the read. This read refusal is separate from coordination_retry and never retries or applies a write. Routine task/dish/goal/attendance workspace writes do not cause a restart. Unknown/unavailable state never yields an empty complete target.

Prefer one bounded response for the first implementation. The accepted technical caps remain safeguards rather than selected production limits: at most 20 stores and 2000 rows per supported family/store, with existing byte and mapping limits enforced. If serialization or required target context exceeds its bound, return a fixed narrower-scope/review obligation, no truncated successful response. One unsupported or oversized family cannot masquerade as complete; independent source-family availability remains explicit.

If pagination is later necessary, it requires a separately reviewed coherent server snapshot/continuation protocol bound to person/session/organization/scope/date range/context. Independent READ COMMITTED pages plus a global workspace fence are insufficient. Do not add durable employee snapshots while S05 retention is pending.

## Coordination, audit and privacy

Future designation/identity writes must use the shared lock order and explicit definitions from the identity/coordination designs, not regex patches or custom triggers on provider-managed auth.sessions. Coordinate the affected organization/store/person scopes before mutation; revalidate that scope set after locks. Deletes and eligibility-driven revocations must coordinate while their parent context still exists. SQLSTATE 40001 retries the whole transaction with the same frozen request; preserve HTTP 503 coordination_retry. Provider side effects do not belong in that transaction retry callback.

Access changes advance relevant monotonic policy/designation/identity fences, while semantic no-ops preserve them. They are not routine workspace changes and must not invalidate unrelated attendance/correction drains. Changing roster eligibility still follows the existing relevant-column input/schedule invalidation rules. Certification freshness at future application time must be checked against current authorized context; a former reviewer's historical signature is audit evidence, not present application authority.

Audit includes actor/target person UUIDs, store/family, action, before/after revision/state, reason, time and receipt identity. Current display names come from the shared person repository; retained entry names are labelled name at time of entry. Preserve history if employment/login ends. Final audit/log retention, reader visibility, source-file custody and production bounds remain S05/I08 decisions; no automatic deletion interval is selected here.

Target responses and private workbooks/reports stay in owner-controlled storage under the accepted custody boundary. No public cache, browser localStorage/sessionStorage employee export, report attachment to PRs, plaintext credentials, contact/payroll data or raw input in logs. Public fixtures are wholly fictional. Error codes are fixed and data-free. Ordinary runtime credentials expose reviewed functions only; server privilege does not make caller-supplied scope authoritative.
